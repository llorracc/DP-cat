## 2. The deck, slide by slide (24 slides)

### 1. Title
**The free category of a dynamic program.** Dynamic programming as functors out of a free category.
Mathematics: Akshay Shanker (DP-cat, 2026). Distilled by C. Carroll for E. Riehl's group. Link to
akshayshanker.github.io/DP-cat. One line: "Everything in CTiC ch. 1–6 is assumed; the economics is
defined on slide 3."

### 2. The question
Economists write a dynamic program as first-order equations between named quantities,
$a=\sigma(m)$, $m'=Ra+\xi'$. What a solver computes is a higher-order object, the Bellman operator
$\mathbb{T}$ on a space of value functions. A first-order syntax tree cannot type $\mathbb{T}$ or carry
the variables that $\max$ and $\mathbb{E}$ bind. **Proposal:** the operator is not a term; it is the
image of an *arrow of a free category* under a functor. Then: syntax = a quiver over a signature;
semantics = functors out of its free category; the structure economists use informally (stages,
post-decision states, "factored programs") = natural transformations, restriction along ordinal
inclusions, and an image factorization; and Yoneda says which operations are the natural ones.

### 3. The economics, once
A sequential decision problem: state spaces $X_j$ (measurable), a transition kernel per equation, a
feasible correspondence $\mathcal D$, rewards $r$, discount $\beta\in(0,1)$, a fixed shock law $\nu$.
A **policy** $\sigma$ is a measurable selection of $\mathcal D$. **Value functions** $v\in L^{X}$,
measurable $X\to\bar{\mathbb R}$. The **policy operator** $T_\sigma v = r_\sigma+\beta K_\sigma^{*}v$,
with $K^{*}v(y)\coloneqq\int K(y,dy')\,v(y')$; the **Bellman operator**
$\mathbb{T}v=\sup_\sigma T_\sigma v$, the value function is its fixed point.
**Example A (buffer stock), used throughout.** Cash $m$, savings $a=\sigma(m)$, next cash
$m'=Ra+\xi'$, reward $u(m-\sigma(m))$:
$(\mathbb{T}v)(m)=\sup_{0<c\le m}\{u(c)+\beta\,\mathbb{E}\,v(R(m-c)+\xi')\}$.
Footnote: Sargent–Stachurski (2026) for kernels and the backward operator (their Markov operator).

### 4. Signatures and declarations (Session B)
**Signature:** a quiver $\mathcal S$ whose vertices are **types** and edges **symbols**
$\mathtt s\colon\mathtt X\to\mathtt X'$; a subset of **decision symbols**, each with a nonempty set
$\Sigma_{\mathtt s}$ of **policies**. $\mathcal S_\Sigma$: same vertices, an edge $(\mathtt s,\sigma)$
for each policy of each decision symbol and one edge $(\mathtt s,\ast)$ otherwise; projection
$p\colon\mathcal S_\Sigma\to\mathcal S$.
**Declaration:** a quiver $Q$ of **fields** with a quiver map $\tau\colon Q\to\mathcal S$ (the typing),
i.e. an object of $\mathsf{Quiver}/\mathcal S$.
⚠ $Q_\Sigma\coloneqq Q\times_{\mathcal S}\mathcal S_\Sigma$: decision edges replaced by
$\Sigma$-indexed parallel families.
**Example A:** types $\mathtt{Xm},\mathtt{Xa}$; $\mathtt{save}\colon\mathtt{Xm}\to\mathtt{Xa}$
(decision), $\mathtt{income}\colon\mathtt{Xa}\to\mathtt{Xm}$; over $N$ periods $Q$ is the chain
$m_0\to a_0\to m_1\to\cdots\to a_{N-1}$. [asset: `fs-period-chain.svg`]
Footnote: GTWW 1977 for sorts and signatures; fields are Backus's atoms.

### 5. Models
$F\dashv U\colon\mathsf{Cat}\rightleftarrows\mathsf{Quiver}$ (Example 4.1.13). **Terms** are the arrows
of $F\mathcal S_\Sigma$ (paths of symbols). An **algebra** of the signature in $\mathsf{C}$ is a functor
$F\mathcal S_\Sigma\to\mathsf{C}$; a **model** of a declaration is a functor $D\colon FQ_\Sigma\to\mathsf{C}$,
equivalently a quiver map $Q_\Sigma\to U\mathsf{C}$: carriers for fields, arrows for edges, no equations
(Akshay's Proposition 1 is this adjunction). Models form $\mathsf{C}^{FQ_\Sigma}$; their morphisms are
natural transformations. ⚠ An algebra induces a model by restriction along
$F(\mathrm{pr}_2)\colon FQ_\Sigma\to F\mathcal S_\Sigma$.
Remark: with no equations and no products this is functorial semantics in its most elementary form; a
model is a representation of the quiver $Q_\Sigma$ in $\mathsf{C}$. Products enter only with equations
that read several fields (slide 22). [asset: `fs-triangle.svg`]

### 6. The forward model
$\mathsf{Stoch}\coloneqq\mathsf{Meas}_{\mathrm{Prob}}$, the Kleisli category of the Giry monad
(Example 5.2.11(iv)); a Markov category in Fritz's sense. $D_f\colon FQ_\Sigma\to\mathsf{Stoch}$: a field
$j\mapsto$ its state space $X_j$; a decision edge $(e,\sigma)\mapsto\delta_\sigma$, the deterministic
kernel of the policy; a policy-free edge $\mapsto$ its transition kernel.
**Example A:** $m,m'\mapsto(0,\infty)$, $a\mapsto[0,\infty)$; $d_\sigma\mapsto\delta_\sigma$;
$\ell\mapsto P$, $P(a,B)=\nu\{\xi: Ra+\xi\in B\}$.
⚠ Laws: $\mathrm{Prob}(X)=\mathsf{Stoch}(\ast,X)$, so push-forward along a path is postcomposition:
the represented functor $\mathsf{Stoch}(\ast,-)$ restricted along $D_f$.

### 7. The backward model
$D_b\colon(FQ_\Sigma)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Set}$: $j\mapsto L^{X_j}$; an edge $(e,\sigma)$ with forward
kernel $K$ $\mapsto$ $(v\mapsto r_{e,\sigma}+\beta_e K^{*}v)$, an affine map $L^{X_i}\to L^{X_j}$
against the edge; $(K'K)^{*}=K^{*}K'^{*}$ makes this a presheaf on $FQ_\Sigma$. The period
$m_t\to a_t\to m_{t+1}$ goes to $T_\sigma=D_b(d_\sigma)\,D_b(\ell)$: **the policy operator is the
image of a path.** [asset: `fs-three-rows.svg`: top row $Q_\Sigma$, middle $D_f$ along, bottom $D_b$
against; no vertical arrows, so no equation is asserted.]
Remark: the reward-free $D_b$ is $\mathsf{Meas}(-,\bar{\mathbb R})$ extended to Kleisli arrows by the
barycentre, where the integral exists; with rewards it is affine in $v$.

### 8. How the two models talk
They have different domains and codomains, so no natural transformation between them is defined.
The pairing $\langle\mu,v\rangle\coloneqq\int v\,d\mu$ satisfies
$\langle\mu K,v\rangle=\langle\mu,K^{*}v\rangle$ (Fubini), i.e. $\mu\mapsto\langle\mu,-\rangle$ is a
natural transformation $\mathsf{Stoch}(\ast,-)D_f\Rightarrow\mathsf{Set}(D_b(-),\bar{\mathbb R})$,
the analogue of evaluation into the double dual (Example 1.4.4(i)); equivalently the pairings form a
**dinatural** transformation (footnote 34, Exercise 1.4.viii). With rewards the identity becomes
affine and is no longer dinaturality. Finite case: $(\mu K)v=\mu(Kv)$.

### 9. Chains, ordinals, groupings (Session C)
A **chain** is a declaration whose quiver is $0\to1\to\cdots\to N$. Fix one policy at each decision
edge; the free category is the ordinal $[N]$ (Riehl's $\mathbb{N}{+}\mathbb{1}$, Example 4.1.14).
⚠ A **grouping** $j_0<\cdots<j_n$ is a monomorphism $\pi\colon[n]\rightarrowtail[N]$, a composite of
cofaces $d^i$; the **grouped model** is the restriction $\pi^{*}D=D\pi$. A **stage structure** is a
grouping that fixes both ends. Grouping changes the indexing category, so it is not a natural
transformation. **Example A:** the chain $m_0,a_0,m_1,\ldots$ has $2N-1$ edges.

### 10. Stages and stagings
A **run** is a path of policy-free edges. A **stage** is an edge of the grouped quiver whose path contains
exactly one decision edge: arrival field → run → decision edge → run → continuation field. A **staging**
is a stage structure all of whose edges are stages: one stage per decision. Boundaries move across runs,
never across decision edges. **Lemma.** A chain with $n_d$ decision edges and runs of $n_k$ edges between
them has $\prod_{k=1}^{n_d-1}(n_k+1)$ stagings. [assets: `fs-stagings.svg`, `fs-consport.svg` for
Example E: two decisions per period, two stagings.]
The backward model of a stage: $T_\sigma = U\cdot D_b(e,\sigma)\cdot F$, with the **continuation map**
$F$ and **arrival map** $U$ the operators of the two runs, independent of $\sigma$: the **stage form**.
(This is the arrival–decision–continuation structure of the Bellman project.)

### 11. Sargent–Stachurski's factored programs are naturality squares
A **factored program** $(V,F,\hat V,\{G_\sigma\})$: $T_\sigma\coloneqq G_\sigma F$,
$\hat T_\sigma\coloneqq FG_\sigma$, so $FT_\sigma=\hat T_\sigma F$. For each $\sigma$,
$n\mapsto T_\sigma^{\,n}$ and $n\mapsto\hat T_\sigma^{\,n}$ are functors $\mathsf{B}\mathbb{N}\to\mathsf{Set}$
and $F$ is a natural transformation between them (induction on $n$ is pasting, Lemma 1.6.11).
**Proposition 5.** On a chain of periods with $T_{t,\sigma}=G_{t,\sigma}F_{t+1}$, the $F_t$ are the
components of $F\colon D\Rightarrow\hat D$ to the **reduced model**
$\hat D(t,\sigma)\coloneqq F_tG_{t,\sigma}$, and
$F_0T_{0,\sigma_0}\cdots T_{N-1,\sigma_{N-1}}=\hat D(0,\sigma_0)\cdots\hat D(N-1,\sigma_{N-1})F_N$.
[asset: `fs-chain-square.svg`]

### 12. Moving a boundary is whiskering
**Proposition 4.** For groupings $\pi,\pi'\colon[n]\rightarrowtail[N]$ with $\pi\le\pi'$ pointwise, the
arrows $D(\pi i\to\pi' i)$ are the components of a natural transformation $\pi^{*}D\Rightarrow\pi'^{*}D$.
⚠ Proof in one line: $[N]$ is a poset, so $\pi\le\pi'$ *is* the unique 2-cell $\pi\Rightarrow\pi'$;
whisker it with $D$ (Remark 1.7.6). For the contravariant $D_b$ the inequality is read in the reversed
order. **Example A:** moving every interior boundary from cash $m_{t+1}$ to savings $a_t$ gives
$D_b(\text{cash staging})\Rightarrow D_b(\text{savings staging})$ with components the income
expectation $D_b(\ell_t)$. [asset: `fs-staging-a1.svg`/`a2`]

### 13. Transforming some fields
**Proposition 6.** Given a model $D$ and surjections $\varphi_j\colon D(j)\twoheadrightarrow B_j$ for
$j\in J$ (identities elsewhere), there is a model $D'$ with $\varphi\colon D\Rightarrow D'$ natural iff
for every edge $(e,\sigma)\colon j\to i$ with $j\in J$:
$\varphi_j(v)=\varphi_j(v')\Rightarrow\varphi_i(D(e,\sigma)v)=\varphi_i(D(e,\sigma)v')$.
Then $D'$ is unique: $D'(e,\sigma)(\varphi_jv)=\varphi_i(D(e,\sigma)v)$.
⚠ In words: $\varphi_i D(e,\sigma)$ coequalizes the kernel pair of $\varphi_j$, so $D(e,\sigma)$
descends to the quotient. Locality: one field, one edge at a time.

### 14. The coarsest cut is an image factorization
For a family $T_\sigma\colon V\to W$ ($\sigma\in\Sigma$), a **cut** is $(\hat V,F,G_\sigma)$ with
$T_\sigma=G_\sigma F$, $F$ independent of $\sigma$; **coarser** means $F=\omega F'$ on $F'(V)$.
**Theorem 7.** Let $v\sim v'$ iff $T_\sigma v=T_\sigma v'$ for all $\sigma$, $q\colon V\twoheadrightarrow\bar V\coloneqq V/{\sim}$,
$\bar G_\sigma[v]\coloneqq T_\sigma v$. Then $(\bar V,q,\bar G_\sigma)$ is a cut, and for every cut there is
exactly one $\omega_F\colon F(V)\to\bar V$ with $q=\omega_F F$; it is onto and $\bar G_\sigma\omega_F=G_\sigma$.
Hence a coarsest cut exists and is unique up to a unique bijection.
⚠ **Proof.** $(\bar V,q)$ is the image factorization of $\langle T_\sigma\rangle_\sigma\colon V\to W^{\Sigma}$
(Example 3.6.11); a cut is a factorization of this map through $F$, and the image is the smallest such.
$\square$
**Example A:** $v\sim v'$ iff $\mathbb{E}\,v(Ra+\xi')=\mathbb{E}\,v'(Ra+\xi')$ for all $a$: $q$ is the
income expectation, and $\bar V$ is the economists' **post-decision value function** on savings.
Not injective when $\nu$ has a density, so this is a genuine quotient, not a natural isomorphism; its
use is exactly that it discards what no policy operator can distinguish.

### 15. The canonical stage form
$W_\Sigma\coloneqq\bigcup_\sigma T_\sigma(V)\subseteq W$ with inclusion $U_\Sigma$. Every stage form
$T_\sigma=UG_\sigma F$ is linked to $T_\sigma=U_\Sigma\bar G_\sigma q$ by the single map $\omega_F$:
$q=\omega_F F$ on $V$ and $U_\Sigma\bar G_\sigma\omega_F=UG_\sigma$ on $F(V)$. ⚠ Dually, $U_\Sigma$ is
the image of the copairing $[T_\sigma]_\sigma\colon\Sigma\times V\to W$, and every factorization
$T_\sigma=U\tilde G_\sigma$ with $U$ monic factors through it (Akshay's Proposition 8).
Every policy operator of a stage is order-preserving for the pointwise order, each factor being so.
Open: the family $\{T_\sigma\}$ determines neither $F$ nor $U$ in the declaration; $q$ is the
coarsest $F$, $U_\Sigma$ the finest $U$. [asset: `fs-canonical.svg`]

### 16. From backward to forward
From a factorization $K=K_2K_1$ of a kernel one gets $K^{*}=K_1^{*}K_2^{*}$, and a grouping groups both
models alike ($\pi^{*}D_f$, $(\pi^{\mathrm{op}})^{*}D_b$). Not conversely: a cut's $F$ may be any map of
sets ($Fv=v^3$). On bounded functions, a map is $K^{*}$ for a kernel $K$ iff it is linear, positive,
unital and monotone-continuous; then $K(x,B)=(T\mathbf 1_B)(x)$. For a stage, $v\sim v'$ iff
$K_\sigma^{*}v=K_\sigma^{*}v'$ for all $\sigma$, and $q$ is the backward operator of the kernel
$J((\sigma,x),-)\coloneqq K_\sigma(x,-)$ on pairs (policy, arrival state), onto its image. In Example A
the reduced model is itself a backward model on functions of savings.

### 17. Policies are sections; policy insertion is よ
$E\coloneqq\{(s,b):b\in\mathcal D(s)\}\xrightarrow{p}S$; sections $\iota$ of $p$ are exactly the feasible
policies, $\iota_\sigma(s)=(s,\sigma(s))$. In the reward-free backward model the decision operator is
$\iota_\sigma^{*}\colon\mathsf{Set}(E,Z)\to\mathsf{Set}(S,Z)$, $w\mapsto w\iota_\sigma$.
**Proposition 10.** A family $\Phi_Z\colon\mathsf{Set}(E,Z)\to\mathsf{Set}(S,Z)$ natural in $Z$ is
$\iota^{*}$ for exactly one $\iota\colon S\to E$, namely $\iota=\Phi_E(\mathrm{id}_E)$; $\iota$ is a
section iff $\Phi_S(p)=\mathrm{id}_S$. (Corollary 2.2.8 with $c=E$, $c'=S$.)
Expectation is *not* natural in the value set (fails for $z\mapsto z^2$); it is natural for affine maps,
since probabilities integrate to one. [asset: `fs-section.svg`]

### 18. Rules, and why the stage form is the only one
For $\mathsf{C}$ locally small and objects $c_0,\ldots,c_3$, a **rule** is a family
$\alpha_P\colon P(c_1,c_2)\to P(c_0,c_3)$, one for each bifunctor
$P\colon\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{Set}$, natural in $P$.
**Proposition 11.** Every rule is $\alpha_P=P(F,U)$ for exactly one $F\colon c_0\to c_1$,
$U\colon c_2\to c_3$. ⚠ The Yoneda argument applied to the representable
$(\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathsf{C})((c_1,c_2),-)=\mathsf{C}(-,c_1)\times\mathsf{C}(c_2,-)$ and its
element $(\mathrm{id}_{c_1},\mathrm{id}_{c_2})$; the proof of Theorem 2.2.4 goes through although
$\mathsf{Set}^{\mathsf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathsf{C}}$ need not be locally small. On the hom bifunctor
the rule sends $G$ to $UGF$: the stage form is the form of every rule.

### 19. What the lemma does and does not do
Does: Propositions 10 and 11 characterize the operations inside a stage that are natural in the value
set and in the bifunctor: precomposition with a section, and conjugation by fixed maps. The initiality
of $(\mathbb{N},s,0)$ (Example 2.1.1) and of $(FQ,\eta)$ (Proposition 1) are initial-object statements;
the lemma adds (Proposition 2.4.8) that $0$ is a universal element.
Does not: choose a factorization ($F=U=\mathrm{id}$ is one); produce $q$, which comes from images,
not from the lemma; by Proposition 6, $q$ is the coarsest admissible component at one field.

### 20. Where this sits ⚠
- Functorial semantics (Lawvere 1963; Riehl §5.5): models as functors; here with no equations and no
  products, i.e. quiver representations. Classifying categories of type theories (Jacobs 1999) for
  the syntax-to-semantics step.
- Markov categories (Fritz 2020): $\mathsf{Stoch}$ and the Giry monad.
- Open games and optics: Ghani–Hedges–Winschel–Zahn, *Compositional game theory*, LICS 2018;
  Hedges & Rodríguez Sakamoto, *Value iteration is optic composition*, ACT 2022 (arXiv:2206.04547)
  and *Reinforcement learning in categorical cybernetics*, EPTCS 429 (2025; arXiv:2404.02688):
  Bellman updates as (parametrised) optics, with a representable functor doing the work.
- Bakirtzis–Savvas–Topcu, *Categorical semantics of compositional reinforcement learning*, JMLR 2025:
  a category of MDPs with pushouts for task composition.
- Mahadevan, *Universal decisions with Kan extensions*.
Honest positioning, which is Akshay's own view of the work: the novelty is in the applied mathematics,
not in the category theory. The contribution is a syntax whose semantics is a functor, and the
recognition of the economists' constructions as the categorical ones above.

### 21. Not yet done
Several inputs: an equation reading several fields is not an edge; symbols need arities, interpreted
by products of state spaces, and the product of measurable spaces is not a categorical product in
$\mathsf{Stoch}$ (a kernel into $X\times Y$ is not its two marginals). Branching: a discrete choice
among continuations reads several fields; it is where Akshay expects initial diagrams to matter most.
Dimension: which declarations have coarsest cuts on functions of fewer
variables. Choice of staging among the $\prod_k(n_k+1)$.

### 22. Questions for a category theorist ⚠
1. Is "declaration = quiver over a signature quiver; models = functors out of $F(Q\times_{\mathcal S}\mathcal S_\Sigma)$"
   the right syntax, or should it be a sketch / finite-product theory from the start, to admit arities
   and branching?
2. The backward model is a presheaf on $FQ_\Sigma$ built from $\mathsf{Meas}(-,\bar{\mathbb R})$ via
   the Giry monad. Is there a clean statement (Kleisli extension against the barycentre algebra;
   a profunctor; a dinatural/extranatural formulation) that also carries the rewards?
3. The Bellman operator $\mathbb{T}=\bigvee_\sigma T_\sigma$ is a join over a parallel family. Is the
   right setting $\mathsf{Pos}$-enriched (or $\mathsf{Sup}$-enriched) models, with optimality a
   colimit-like operation on hom-posets?
4. Stagings are restrictions along cofaces. Do $\mathrm{Lan}_\pi$ and $\mathrm{Ran}_\pi$ (Ch. 6) have
   economic meaning, e.g. canonical refinements or coarsenings of a stage structure?
5. The coarsest cut is an image in $\mathsf{Set}$. In which categories of value functions (measurable,
   bounded, $L^p$) does the (epi, mono) factorization still give the economists' reduction?
6. For branching: tree-shaped quivers, colimits, or initial algebras of polynomial functors?
7. Is $\int D_b$ (objects: a field with a value function; morphisms: policy paths realizing it)
   a useful object? Backward induction walks in it.
